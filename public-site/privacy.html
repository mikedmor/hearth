Privacy policy

For Hearth, a home's own start page, and its use of Google Calendar.

Who runs it

Hearth is software a household runs on its own server, at home. That household is the only one with your data. There is no company behind it, no account with anyone else, and nothing is sold or shared.

What Hearth gets from Google

When you connect a Google account to Hearth, you allow it to:

  • see the list of your calendars, and the events in the ones you choose to show;
  • add, change and delete events, only when you do so in Hearth.

Hearth doesn't ask for your email, contacts, files or anything else, and it never reads events from calendars you didn't choose.

Where it's kept

  • Google's permission for Hearth (a "refresh token") is stored encrypted on the home server. It is never shown on a page, not even to the household's admin.
  • Events are fetched when you look at your calendar and kept briefly in the server's memory, so the page is quick. They aren't copied anywhere else.
  • You choose who in the household sees each calendar or event. Nobody else does, and visitors to the home's network see none of it.

This website

This public site has no tracking, analytics, cookies or forms. Its return page receives Google's one-time sign-in code in the address and immediately sends your browser to Hearth on your home network. The code is useless without secrets that only the home server has, and this site doesn't store or send it anywhere.

Stopping it

Disconnect the account in Hearth (Calendar → Add a calendar → Your accounts), or remove Hearth's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Either way, Hearth forgets the account and can no longer reach your calendars.

Google's rules

Hearth's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
